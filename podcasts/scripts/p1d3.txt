# Unit 3: The Great Itemizing Debate and Other Credits
(Season 1. Autumn 2025 in the story. Studio A. Milo has hung a disco ball in the closet. It cost $4.)
[theme]
MILO: Ledger Falls, welcome back to Unplugged! I'm Milo Marsh, and this is Unit 3: deductions and credits. The fun part. Where the money comes BACK.
PENNY: I'm Penny Pocket. Milo bought a disco ball for the closet.
MILO: It was four dollars at the swap meet, Penny.
PENNY: It's a Christmas ornament covered in foil.
MILO: Which makes it festive AND deductible.
ACE: It's not deductible.
MILO: Ace Okafor, enrolled agent, crushing dreams since he passed all three parts of the exam. Ace, why do you have three bags today?
ACE: One for highlighters, one for tabs, and one for backup highlighters. Credits are a two-bag topic. Plus one for emergencies.
PENNY: What's the emergency?
ACE: Someone might use a pen that isn't teal.
MILO: Episode 3.1 was our first married fight. To itemize or not to itemize.
PENNY: It wasn't a fight. It was a debate. With slides.
ACE: The 2025 standard deduction is $15,750 single or married filing separately, $23,625 head of household, and $31,500 married filing jointly. Add $1,600 for each married person who's sixty-five or older or blind, or $2,000 if single or head of household. And if a married couple files separately and one itemizes, both must.
MILO: So the rule is: add up Schedule A. Beat $31,500 or don't bother.
ACE: That's literally the memory hook. Good job, Milo.
MILO: I'm a natural.
PENNY: You read it off my pillow.
ACE: Medical expenses are deductible only to the extent unreimbursed costs exceed seven point five percent of AGI. That includes premiums paid with after-tax money, prescriptions, and twenty-one cents a mile for medical driving in 2025. It doesn't include cosmetic procedures, over-the-counter drugs except insulin, or anything reimbursed or paid with pre-tax money.

(The shoebox on the shelf bursts open. Receipts fly everywhere. Examiner Hoot sits in the middle, wearing them like a nest.)
HOOT: Hoo-hoo-HOLD it. Who here is deducting a teeth whitening kit?
PENNY: Hoot! Were you in my shoebox?
HOOT: Examiner Hoot. Your shoebox contains four hundred receipts, a coupon for soup, and a gum wrapper from 2019. I have filed a report about the gum wrapper.
PENNY: The gum wrapper has sentimental value!
HOOT: The gum wrapper has NO tax value. Teeth whitening is cosmetic. Not deductible.
MILO: Hooty, you look great in receipts.
HOOT: Not. Hooty. Now. State and local taxes. SALT. For 2025, income or sales tax, plus real estate tax, plus personal property tax, capped at $40,000, or $20,000 married filing separately. The cap is reduced by thirty percent of modified AGI over $500,000, or $250,000 for married filing separately, but not below $10,000, or $5,000 married filing separately.
ACE: Mortgage interest. Deductible on up to $750,000 of acquisition debt, $375,000 married filing separately, or $1 million for debt from before December 16, 2017. It covers a main home and one second home. Home equity interest only counts if the loan was used to buy, build or improve the home. And investment interest is deductible up to net investment income.
PENNY: And charity! I gave $300 to Pastor Gail's food pantry.
ACE: For itemizers, cash gifts to public charities are deductible up to sixty percent of AGI. Appreciated long-term capital gain property, up to thirty percent, at fair market value. Excess carries over five years. Any single gift of $250 or more needs a written acknowledgment from the charity.
HOOT: Noncash gifts over $500: Form 8283. Over $5,000: a qualified appraisal. And there is no deduction for the value of your services.
MILO: So the four hours I spent being the pantry's mascot...
HOOT: Are not deductible. They are, however, a matter of public record. There are photos, Milo Marsh.
ACE: Personal casualty losses are deductible only for federally declared disasters, minus $100 per event and ten percent of AGI. Gambling losses up to winnings are still allowed. And the old two-percent-floor miscellaneous deductions are gone.
ACE: Now, new for 2025 through 2028, whether you itemize or not: a senior deduction of $6,000 for each person sixty-five or older, phasing out over $75,000 of modified AGI, or $150,000 joint, and married people must file jointly. Interest on a loan for a new U.S.-assembled car, up to $10,000, phasing out over $100,000, or $200,000 joint. And the tips and overtime deductions from Unit 2.
PENNY: And the verdict on our debate?
ACE: Standard wins. Your Schedule A came to $19,000, and the standard deduction is $31,500.
PENNY: I made slides for nothing.
MILO: The slides were beautiful, babe. They had clip art of a mortgage.
[ding]
MILO: Segment two! Milo's twenty percent treat! I finished my first full year of Milo's Dog Walking. And I heard there's a twenty percent deduction.
ACE: The qualified business income deduction. Up to twenty percent of qualified business income from sole proprietorships, partnerships and S corporations, for individuals, trusts and estates. It's below the line, so you get it whether or not you itemize. But it doesn't reduce AGI or self-employment tax.
MILO: So I take twenty percent of everything I made.
HOOT: Hoo. No. QBI is net U.S. business income, AFTER the deductible half of self-employment tax, self-employed health insurance, and self-employed retirement contributions. And employee wages, S corporation reasonable compensation, guaranteed payments, capital gains, interest and dividends are NOT QBI. QBI losses carry forward.
ACE: Then the cap. The deduction can't exceed twenty percent of taxable income before the QBI deduction, minus net capital gain, and net capital gain includes qualified dividends. Below the threshold, you get the lesser of twenty percent of QBI, plus REIT and PTP income, or that cap.
PENNY: Twenty percent, but the smaller of two twenties.
ACE: Exactly. The 2025 threshold is $197,300, or $394,600 married filing jointly, with a phase-in range of $50,000, or $100,000 joint. At or below the threshold, there are no wage limits and no specified service rules, and you use Form 8995. Above it, Form 8995-A.
HOOT: Specified service trades or businesses. Health, law, accounting, consulting, financial services, performing arts, athletics, brokerage, and any business whose main asset is reputation or skill. Full deduction below the threshold. Partial in the phase-in. NONE above $247,300, or $494,600 joint.
MILO: Is dog walking a specified service? My main asset is my reputation. Dogs love me.
HOOT: Dogs love everyone, Milo Marsh. It is not a professional credential.
[bark]
MILO: Biscuit says it is.
HOOT: Biscuit is not a credentialed witness.
ACE: And above the threshold, the deduction for each business is limited to the greater of fifty percent of its W-2 wages, or twenty-five percent of W-2 wages plus two and a half percent of the unadjusted basis of qualified property.
[stamp]
MILO: Segment three! Lulu!
LULU: Hello. I am Lulu. I am a qualifying relative, and Cousin Dee says I'm worth a fortune.
DEE: Can you hear me now? The child tax credit went up! Lulu's worth $2,200 to Penny!
HOOT: Hoo-hoo-HOLD it.
ACE: For 2025, the child tax credit is $2,200 per qualifying child who is under seventeen at the end of the year, claimed as a dependent, with a Social Security number valid for work. The taxpayer, or at least one spouse on a joint return, needs an SSN too. It's on Schedule 8812.
LULU: But I'm not Penny's qualifying child. I'm her qualifying relative.
ACE: Right. So Lulu gets the credit for other dependents. That's $500, nonrefundable, for each dependent who doesn't qualify for the child tax credit: qualifying relatives, children seventeen or older, and children with ITINs. The dependent must be a U.S. citizen, national or resident.
LULU: So I'm worth five hundred dollars.
PENNY: On a tax return! At home, you're priceless!
LULU: Can I have the five hundred dollars?
PENNY: No.
LULU: Then I'd like to speak to my representative.
ACE: I'm an enrolled agent, Lulu. I'm technically a representative.
LULU: Great. Ace, get me my five hundred dollars.
ACE: The additional child tax credit is the refundable part, up to $1,700 per child for 2025, figured as fifteen percent of earned income over $2,500. And the child tax credit plus the other dependent credit is reduced by $50 for each $1,000 of modified AGI over $200,000, or $400,000 joint.
HOOT: Qualifying child: $2,200. Everyone else you claim: $500.
DEE: Oh no, Lulu, I'm sorry, I thought you were worth...
[glitch]
LULU: She cut out on purpose. She does that.
ACE: The child and dependent care credit, for 2025. It covers care for a qualifying child under thirteen, or a spouse or dependent who can't care for themselves, so the taxpayer can work. Expenses up to $3,000 for one person or $6,000 for two or more, limited to the lower-earning spouse's earned income. The credit is thirty-five percent, going down to twenty percent once AGI is over $43,000. Form 2441.
PENNY: Our nanny Joy counts?
ACE: Daycare, a nanny, day camp, and before- and after-school care count. Overnight camp doesn't. Kindergarten and higher tuition doesn't. And payments to your spouse, your dependent, or your child under nineteen don't count. Excluded employer dependent care benefits, up to $5,000, reduce the expense limit. You need the provider's TIN, and married filing separately generally can't claim it.
MILO: So if I paid Lulu to watch Biscuit...
LULU: I charge eight dollars an hour.
ACE: Lulu's your dependent, and Biscuit's not a qualifying person. Twice no.
HOOT: Adoption credit, 2025. Up to $17,280 per child, up to $5,000 of it refundable. It phases out between $259,190 and $299,190 of modified AGI. Form 8839. For a special-needs child, the full credit is allowed regardless of expenses.
[ding]
MILO: Segment four! Back to school! Penny's taking accounting at Ledger Falls Community College, and I'm taking a pottery class.
PENNY: For your career?
MILO: For my soul, Penny. And my mug business.
ACE: Two education credits. The American opportunity tax credit: up to $2,500 per student, forty percent refundable, for the first four years of college, in a degree program, at least half-time. The lifetime learning credit: up to $2,000 per return, nonrefundable, for any number of years. One credit per student per year. Form 8863.
HOOT: AOTC: one hundred percent of the first $2,000 of qualified expenses plus twenty-five percent of the next $2,000, for a maximum of $2,500, forty percent refundable. The student must be in a degree program, at least half-time for one academic period, not have completed the first four years, not have claimed AOTC for more than four years, and have no felony drug conviction.
ACE: The lifetime learning credit is twenty percent of up to $10,000 of expenses per return, so a max of $2,000, nonrefundable. Any number of years, no half-time or degree requirement. Even one course to improve job skills counts.
MILO: My pottery class improves my job skills! I'm in the mug business!
HOOT: Your mug business has sold one mug, Milo Marsh. To your wife.
PENNY: It leaks. I use it for pens.
ACE: Qualified expenses are tuition and required fees. For AOTC, required books and supplies count even if you buy them somewhere else. For the lifetime learning credit, only if paid to the school. Room, board, insurance and transportation don't count. And you reduce expenses by tax-free scholarships and grants.
ACE: Both credits phase out between $80,000 and $90,000 of modified AGI, or $160,000 to $180,000 married filing jointly. None for married filing separately. If a student is someone's dependent, only the taxpayer who claims them can take the credit, and expenses the student paid count as paid by that taxpayer. The school sends Form 1098-T.
HOOT: No double benefit. The same expenses cannot be used for an education credit AND paid with a tax-free 529 or Coverdell distribution or a scholarship.
ACE: A common move is to pay $4,000 from other funds to max out the AOTC, and use the 529 for the rest. AOTC per student, LLC per return.

ACE: The earned income tax credit for 2025, which is refundable. The maximum is $649 with no qualifying children, $4,328 with one, $7,152 with two, and $8,046 with three or more. And investment income can't be more than $11,950.
HOOT: Rules for everyone. Valid Social Security numbers. Earned income. Investment income of $11,950 or less. A U.S. citizen or resident alien all year. No Form 2555. Not someone else's qualifying child. Married filing separately only if separated: with a qualifying child, and either lived apart the last six months of the year or legally separated.
ACE: An EITC qualifying child has to pass relationship, age, residency and joint return tests. Age: under nineteen, or a student under twenty-four, or disabled, and younger than you. Residency: lived with you in the U.S. for more than half the year. And here's the twist: there's no support test, and the child doesn't need to be your dependent.
LULU: Four tests for the child.
ACE: Without a qualifying child, you must be twenty-five to sixty-four at the end of the year, at least one spouse if filing jointly, have your main home in the U.S. for more than half the year, and not be anyone's dependent or qualifying child. The max is $649.
HOOT: Now. The four duties for the PREPARER. Due diligence, for the EITC, the child tax credit, additional child tax credit and credit for other dependents, the AOTC, and head of household status. One: Form 8867, filed with the return. Two: the computation worksheets. Three: the knowledge requirement. Four: keep records for three years. The penalty applies per failure, per credit.
ACE: The knowledge requirement means you can't ignore what you know. If information seems incorrect, inconsistent or incomplete, you ask, and you document the questions and answers at the time. Otherwise, good-faith reliance on the client is fine.
HOOT: And after an EITC denial that wasn't just a math error, the taxpayer must file Form 8862 to claim it again. If the denial was for reckless or intentional disregard of the rules: a two-year ban. Fraud: a ten-year ban.
MILO: Ten years! That's longer than any of my businesses!

ACE: The residential clean energy credit is thirty percent for solar, wind, geothermal, batteries of at least three kilowatt hours, and fuel cells. No dollar cap, and unused credit carries forward. But it's not available for expenditures made after December 31, 2025, which means installed after that date. Form 5695, Part I.
PENNY: Our panels went up in October. 2025 was the last sunny year.
ACE: The energy efficient home improvement credit: thirty percent for an existing main home, with a $1,200 annual cap. Inside that: windows $600, doors $250 each or $500 total, and a home energy audit $150. Plus a separate $2,000 for heat pumps and biomass. No carryforward, and it ends for property placed in service after 2025. Form 5695, Part II.
HOOT: Clean vehicle credits. New, up to $7,500. Used, up to $4,000. They END for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025.
MILO: I was going to buy an electric scooter in December!
HOOT: A scooter was never a qualifying clean vehicle, Milo Marsh.
ACE: Foreign income taxes. You can take a credit on Form 1116, limited to the U.S. tax on foreign income, with a one-year carryback and ten-year carryforward. Or an itemized deduction. All one way each year. And no credit on income excluded with the foreign earned income exclusion.
HOOT: Skip Form 1116 if all foreign income is passive, reported on 1099s, and foreign tax is $300 or less, $600 joint. But then there's no carryover for that year.
ACE: And the saver's credit: fifty, twenty or ten percent of up to $2,000 of retirement contributions, or $4,000 joint. 2025 AGI caps are $39,500 single, $59,250 head of household, and $79,000 joint. You must be eighteen or older, not a full-time student, and not a dependent. Nonrefundable. Form 8880.
